# AGS Beers Criteria 2023 Summary

## Overview of Geriatric Safety Principles
The American Geriatrics Society (AGS) Beers Criteria provides explicit recommendations regarding medications that are potentially inappropriate for older adults aged 65 and older. Due to age-related physiological changes such as reduced glomerular filtration, decreased hepatic clearance, increased body fat ratio, and heightened central nervous system receptor sensitivity, older patients experience heightened vulnerability to adverse drug events.

## Benzodiazepines and Sedative Hypnotics
Older adults possess marked pharmacodynamic sensitivity to benzodiazepines (e.g., alprazolam, lorazepam, diazepam, clonazepam) and nonbenzodiazepine Z-drug receptor agonists (e.g., zolpidem, eszopiclone). Clinical trials demonstrate significant increases in cognitive impairment, delirium, motor incoordination, falls, fractures, and motor vehicle crashes. Tapering and discontinuation should be prioritized alongside non-pharmacological sleep hygiene interventions.
Source: AGS Beers Criteria (2023 Update) - Benzodiazepine Category

## First-Generation Antihistamines
First-generation H1 antihistamines (e.g., diphenhydramine, chlorpheniramine, promethazine, hydroxyzine) carry profound anticholinergic properties. In elderly patients, these compounds produce xerostomia, urinary retention (particularly in benign prostatic hyperplasia), constipation, blurred vision, acute confusion, and falls. Second-generation non-sedating antihistamines (cetirizine, fexofenadine) are preferred for allergic symptoms.
Source: AGS Beers Criteria (2023 Update) - Anticholinergic Category

## Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)
Chronic systemic NSAID administration should be avoided in older adults due to substantial risks of peptic ulcer formation, gastrointestinal hemorrhage, renal function deterioration, and fluid retention exacerbating heart failure. Concurrent use of multiple NSAIDs provides zero therapeutic advantage while compounding toxicity.
Source: AGS Beers Criteria (2023 Update) - Pain and NSAID Guidance

## Proton Pump Inhibitor Durations
Proton pump inhibitors (pantoprazole, omeprazole, rabeprazole) should generally not be scheduled for durations exceeding 8 weeks unless specific clinical conditions exist (e.g., severe erosive esophagitis, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, or concurrent high-risk chronic NSAID use). Long-term usage increases risks of Clostridioides difficile diarrhea, osteoporotic fractures, hypomagnesemia, and malabsorption.
Source: AGS Beers Criteria (2023 Update) - Gastrointestinal Medications
