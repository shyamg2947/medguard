# Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden and Falls in the Elderly

## Cumulative Anticholinergic Burden
Central and peripheral cholinergic neurotransmission governs memory consolidation, attention, pupil accommodation, gastrointestinal peristalsis, and detrusor muscle tone. Aging is accompanied by progressive loss of central cholinergic neurons and increased blood-brain barrier permeability. Concomitant administration of multiple drugs with even mild anticholinergic effects leads to cumulative toxicity.

## The ACB Scale Scoring Criteria
The Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB) scale quantifies this cumulative exposure:
- **Score 1 (Mild / Serum Anticholinergic Activity)**: Atenolol, captopril, digoxin, furosemide, metoprolol, ranitidine, warfarin, alprazolam.
- **Score 2 (Moderate Burden)**: Amantadine, carbamazepine, clozapine.
- **Score 3 (Definite / Severe Central Anticholinergic Action)**: Amitriptyline, diphenhydramine, promethazine, hydroxyzine, chlorpheniramine, oxybutynin, paroxetine, trihexyphenidyl.

A total ACB score of **3 or higher** is associated with a 50% increase in mortality over 2 years, acute delirium episodes, and a significant rise in recurrent falls and fractures. Deprescribing or substituting high-burden agents is a core geriatric recommendation.
Source: Boustani M, et al. Impact of anticholinergics on cognitive decline. Aging & Mental Health. 2008; Campbell N, et al. J Am Geriatr Soc. 2013
