# Anticoagulant and Antiplatelet Hemorrhage Hazards

## Mechanisms of Bleeding Synergy
Anticoagulants (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran) inhibit the coagulation cascade, preventing fibrin clot stabilization. Antiplatelet agents (aspirin, clopidogrel) impair primary platelet plug formation. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) compound this danger through two distinct mechanisms:
1. Reversible inhibition of platelet cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1), diminishing thromboxane A2 production.
2. Direct topical and systemic suppression of gastric protective prostaglandins, causing mucosal erosions and ulcerations.

## Clinical Risk Quantification
Co-administration of warfarin or DOACs with oral NSAIDs increases major gastrointestinal bleeding incidence by 300% to 500% compared to anticoagulant monotherapy. The combination requires urgent clinical review and substitution with non-ulcerogenic analgesics such as paracetamol, topical NSAIDs, or non-pharmacologic physical modalities.
Source: FDA Warfarin Black Box Warning / ACC Antithrombotic Guidelines (2022)

## Herbal Antithrombotic Interactions
Concentrated botanical supplements such as Ginkgo biloba (ginkgolides), Allium sativum (garlic extract), and Curcuma longa (curcumin/turmeric) demonstrate in vitro and in vivo antiplatelet or mild anticoagulant properties. When co-ingested with prescription blood thinners, unpredictable potentiation of bleeding occurs. Clinicians must actively elicit dietary supplement histories.
Source: NIH NCCIH Botanical Monographs / Natural Medicines Database
