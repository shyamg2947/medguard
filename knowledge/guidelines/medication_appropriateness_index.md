# Medication Appropriateness Index (MAI) Summary

## The 10-Question Evaluative Framework
Developed by Hanlon and colleagues, the Medication Appropriateness Index (MAI) offers an implicit clinical instrument to evaluate the suitability of each drug in an older patient's regimen across ten core dimensions:

1. **Indication**: Is there an active, documented medical indication for the drug?
2. **Effectiveness**: Is the medication effective for the specific medical condition?
3. **Dosage**: Is the dose correct, especially taking into account geriatric renal function?
4. **Directions**: Are the directions practical and correct for the patient?
5. **Practicality**: Are the directions feasible and understandable?
6. **Drug-Drug Interactions**: Are there significant clinically relevant interactions?
7. **Drug-Disease Interactions**: Are there contraindications based on co-existing illnesses?
8. **Duplication**: Is there unnecessary therapeutic duplication across classes or active ingredients?
9. **Duration**: Is the duration of therapy acceptable and not excessively prolonged?
10. **Expense**: Is the medication the least expensive alternative among equally effective options?

Source: Hanlon JT, Schmader KE, et al. A method for assessing drug therapy appropriateness. J Clin Epidemiol. 1992
