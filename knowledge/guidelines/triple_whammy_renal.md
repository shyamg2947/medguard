# The Triple Whammy Acute Kidney Injury Syndrome

## Hemodynamic Mechanism of Renal Collapse
Normal glomerular filtration depends heavily on the transcapillary hydrostatic pressure gradient between the afferent and efferent arterioles:
1. **Diuretics** (loop or thiazide) reduce intravascular plasma volume and cardiac output, decreasing renal blood flow and stimulating intrarenal compensatory mechanisms.
2. **ACE Inhibitors and ARBs** block angiotensin II-mediated constriction of the **efferent** arteriole, dilating the outlet and lowering intraglomerular filtration pressure.
3. **NSAIDs** extinguish local renal prostaglandin synthesis, which is essential to keep the **afferent** arteriole dilated during volume-depleted states.

When all three agents are administered concurrently, afferent inflow is choked off by NSAIDs while efferent outflow remains wide open due to ACEi/ARBs, precipitating an immediate, catastrophic plunge in glomerular filtration and acute tubular necrosis.

## Geriatric Vulnerability and Clinical Management
The Triple Whammy combination accounts for a substantial proportion of drug-induced hospital admissions in geriatric patients. Risk factors include pre-existing chronic kidney disease, volume depletion (dehydration, diarrhea), and hot climates. Patients presenting with this combination require prompt discontinuation of the NSAID, assessment of serum creatinine and electrolytes, and hydration monitoring.
Source: Thomas MC. Diuretics, ACE inhibitors and NSAIDs--the triple whammy. Med J Aust. 2000; Loboz & Shenfield. Br J Clin Pharmacol. 2005
